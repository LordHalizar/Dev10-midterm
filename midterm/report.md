# IDB Analysis of Population Data Report
Using the dataset provided by the International Database, I analyzed population trends and estimates. This was done to identify key patterns in population composition and changes over time.

## Dataset
From the IDB dataset provided, I focused on total population, age-ranges, median age, & yearly data from ten sample countries. Of the countries selected, two were chosen for their high population values, two for the known slower (even negative) population growth, and a number of other countries from Northern America (including the US) and Europe were chosen for comparison.

## Initial Hypothesis
The United States of America has an aging population and life expectancy is increasing.

## Visualizations
### US Data
With my initial research into US population data, I felt confident that my hypothesis would be proven right. This can be seen in how, though slow, a dip appears in total population project data over time. This led me to believe I would likely see an overall decrease in birth rates and an increase in those over older age-ranges. This turned out to be true, as seen in the following projection of age-ranges. But having gotten more subtle results than I thought, I figured it would be a good idea to compare the US against other countrys' population data.

### Population Data
Looking at my larger data set of several countries, I was honestly shocked with my findings. While I got expected results from countries like Japan and South Korea, I got rather surprising results with countries like China. I had initially expected China's population growth would match countries like India with a similarly large initial population, but instead I found there to be a staggering drop off in their overall population in the projection data. This too was reflected in the age-range data where a staggering drop off can be seen in age-ranges 0-4 and stark increases in those above the age of 80. For a final comparison, I decided to do a comparative analysis of median ages across my selected sample countries. And from this data, I found that not only was the United States not experiencing a stark increase in median age, it actually maintained the lowest median age among the cohort of countries!
